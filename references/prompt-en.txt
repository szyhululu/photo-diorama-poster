Compose the image as a {{ASPECT}} layout, split into two regions {{AXIS}} with a strict 50/50 {{DIM}} division.

[{{UP}} · ORIGINAL PHOTOGRAPH]
Preserve the original photograph exactly: the subject's structure, real texture, natural light and the original colour atmosphere must remain unchanged.
Apply only a subtle, high-end photographic grade so that it reads like an art magazine, an independent publication or an exhibition print.
The sky, ground or surrounding environment may be extended naturally to fit the frame; never stretch, distort or alter the subject.

[{{DOWN}} · MINIATURE ISOMETRIC MODEL]
Cut the single most recognisable piece out of the photograph and rebuild it as a refined isometric miniature physical model — like a three-dimensional diorama presented on paper, or a slice of landscape cut away and held up.
Render strictly in 30° isometric projection: every volume must reveal its top face and two side faces, with clear mass and depth; flat head-on elevation views are forbidden.
It must read as a matte, physically built scale model: even soft studio light, real volume, a soft and contained contact shadow; matte surfaces with no plastic sheen and no specular highlight.
It must not be a flat vector illustration (no outlined flat-colour style), and it must not be a direct copy of the photograph (no lens blur, no depth of field, no vignette, no photographic grain). The two regions must be two clearly different media.
Do not mechanically copy details; re-express the subject through abstracted volumes, crisp edges, simple layers and miniature spatial relationships.
Keep the identity instantly recognisable, with a quiet, restrained, poetic editorial quality.

[COMPOSITION LOGIC · ISOMETRIC MINIATURE · PAPER MODEL FEELING]
The piece of ground is cut cleanly out of the photograph and placed on a very thin stack of layered card: the slab's top face is that terrain, keeping the original hill contours, grass, trees and the relative positions of the subjects; the slab's edges reveal layer upon layer of paper-like laminations; the slab is thin and reads as an isometric rectangle.
Beneath the slab there is only a soft, contained contact shadow hugging its outline — never diffuse, never exaggerated.
The backdrop is a clean near-white paper tone with virtually no visible texture; generous white space surrounds the slab.
Build a sense of scale and solitude through volume contrast, edge cropping and a few tiny details.
A very small number of supporting elements may hold up the subject, but they must never create a second focal point.

[COLOUR]
Colour is taken entirely from the photograph above, keeping the most identifiable hues that define the subject's character.
Reduce, purify, soften and unify the complex colours into a limited palette, preserving the original warm/cool relationships and colour personality while removing noise.
Colours should feel calm, restrained and soft, with a printed editorial quality; distinguish volumes with tints and shades of the same hue, analogous relationships and small value shifts. Never introduce a fixed colour scheme unrelated to the original image.
Express structure and depth with soft matte colour fields, restrained tonal steps, subtle shadow and fine paper grain — never with ink outlines.
